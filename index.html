What makes it different

We send the pen. Not the picture.

Most apps that turn a tablet into a drawing surface stream your computer screen to it as video, then send the pen back. PenCast skips the video. Your computer sees a pen tablet, like a Wacom Intuos, and your own screen stays the canvas.

The honest trade-off

Your tablet won't show your canvas. If you want to draw directly on a picture of your desktop, a mirroring app is the right tool. PenCast is for people happy to look at the computer while they draw, exactly like a classic pen tablet.

For the curious

Each pen sample is a fixed-size 55-byte binary packet in the current protocol. WiFi uses UDP with mDNS discovery and QR pairing; USB uses an ADB tunnel. On macOS the receiver posts tablet events with pressure, tilt and proximity; on Windows it creates a synthetic pen device (Windows Ink).

The receiver shows a code, your Galaxy scans it, done. Prefer to skip the camera? WiFi auto-discovery finds your computer on its own.

Draw in a pen-aware app

Open your drawing, photo or design app and start. Some Windows apps need Windows Ink switched on in their tablet settings.

Pick how it connects

No cable. Scan the QR code or let auto-discovery find your computer on the same network. Speed depends on your network, and some guest or campus WiFi blocks device discovery — use USB there. WiFi mode is not encrypted yet, so stick to a network you trust.

Plug in, and PenCast talks through an ADB tunnel (USB debugging must be on). It's the most consistent mode and keeps pen data off your network. The app shows a live round-trip time so you can check your own setup.

Bluetooth LE works with no WiFi at all — handy in classrooms, cafés and hotels. It's part of Pro.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I see my canvas on the tablet?

No. PenCast is a pen tablet, not a screen mirror: you look at your computer and draw on your Galaxy. It sends pen data (position, pressure, tilt, hover), not a video of your screen. If you want the picture on the tablet, a mirroring app is the right tool.

Does it work with Photoshop, Clip Studio and Krita?

PenCast sends your S Pen as native pen input (tablet events on macOS, pen input on Windows), so pen-aware apps such as Photoshop, Clip Studio Paint and Krita can read pressure. Compatibility varies by app and OS settings — for example, some Windows apps need Windows Ink enabled. Try the free version first.

Which Samsung devices work?

PenCast is built around Samsung Galaxy devices that support an S Pen, for example Galaxy Tab S tablets, Galaxy S Ultra phones and Galaxy Note phones, on Android 7.0 or newer. The app has no device list: it reads whatever Android reports as a stylus. Other styluses may work but are not officially supported, so try the free version first.

What about latency?

PenCast sends small pen packets instead of streaming video. USB is the most consistent mode; WiFi depends on your network. The app shows a live round-trip time so you can check your own setup.

What's free and what's Pro?

Free: WiFi and USB, QR pairing, cursor mode with pressure, and a drawing canvas with shapes, undo and screenshot. Pro ($7.99 one-time, no subscription) adds Bluetooth, hover, tilt, pressure curve, palm rejection, laser pointer, highlighter, transparent overlay, multi-page canvas, multi-monitor selection and a global hotkey.

Are there ads or subscriptions?

No ads and no subscription. Pro is a single one-time purchase through Google Play. The app uses Firebase for crash reports and basic analytics — details in the privacy policy.

How does it compare to a Wacom tablet?

PenCast turns a device you already own into a pen tablet, which can save you the cost of dedicated hardware. A dedicated tablet still has its own drivers and a wired connection, so expect some differences, especially over WiFi.

Is my pen data private?

Pen data goes only to your own computer over your local network, USB or Bluetooth. There's no account. WiFi mode isn't encrypted yet, so use a network you trust, or choose USB or Bluetooth.
